\title{\textbf{Scale-Matched Data Consistency for Non-Hot Diffusion Priors:\\
An HQS Framework for Linear Image Restoration}}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present an image-restoration method that couples a \emph{non-hot} diffusion prior with a
scale-matched data-consistency step. The prior's forward blur $\K_t$ and the measurement operator
$\A$ share a fixed orthogonal transform $\Phimat$ that diagonalizes both, which turns each reverse
step into a closed-form MAP update. We give the problem statement, the algorithm, and numerical
results on held-out CelebA-HQ-256 for Gaussian, motion, and defocus deblurring and $\times4$
super-resolution, benchmarked at $n{=}200$ against DPS, DDRM, DiffPIR, and DDNM.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem definition}
\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{Linear inverse problem.}
Let $\x_0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ ($d=3HW$) be a clean image. We observe
\begin{equation}
\y \;=\; \A\,\x_0 \;+\; \bm{n},\qquad \bm{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm 0,\;\sigma_y^2\I),
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $\A$ is a known linear degradation and
$\sigma_y$ the noise level. The task is to recover $\x_0$ from $\y$.

\paragraph{Non-hot diffusion prior.}
A non-hot diffusion prior defines a
\emph{deterministic} forward corruption by progressive blurring,
\begin{equation}
\x_t \;=\; \K_t\,\x_0,\qquad t=0,1,\dots,N,\qquad \K_0=\I,
\label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
where $\K_t$ is a heat-dissipation (Gaussian-smoothing) operator of increasing scale
$\sigma_t$. A network $R_\theta$ is trained to invert one level of blur; its
reverse step produces a prior mean
\begin{equation}
\bmu_{t-1} \;=\; R_\theta(\x_t,\,t)\;\approx\;\mathbb{E}[\x_{t-1}\mid \x_t].
\label{eq:reverse}
\end{equation}
Crucially, $R_\theta$ operates entirely in the \emph{image domain}: it maps blurred pixels
to (a residual toward) less-blurred pixels; the transform basis enters only inside $\K_t$.

\paragraph{Shared eigenbasis.}
Both $\K_t$ and $\A$ are diagonalized by a fixed orthogonal transform $\Phimat$:
\begin{equation}
\K_t=\Phimat^{-1}\,\mathrm{diag}(\hat{k}_t)\,\Phimat,\qquad
\A=\Phimat^{-1}\,\mathrm{diag}(\hat{a})\,\Phimat,\qquad
\hat{k}_t[j]=e^{-\tfrac12\sigma_t^2\lambda_j},
\label{eq:diag}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_j$ the Laplacian eigenvalues. The correct basis depends on boundary
condition and kernel symmetry:
\emph{(i)} \textbf{Neumann/symmetric} blur (Gaussian) $\Rightarrow$ $\Phimat=$ DCT,
$\hat a$ real; \emph{(ii)} \textbf{periodic/asymmetric} blur $\Rightarrow$
$\Phimat=$ DFT, $\hat a$ complex. When $\A$ and $\K_t$ share $\Phimat$
they commute,
\begin{equation}
\A\,\K_t \;=\; \K_t\,\A,
\label{eq:commute}
\end{equation}
which is the property our solver exploits.

\section{Method: per-step MAP via HQS}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Restoration as guided reverse diffusion.}
We run the prior's reverse chain from $t=N$ to $0$ and, at every level, correct the prior
mean $\bmu_{t-1}$ toward the measurement. We interpret the correction as one step of a
\emph{plug-and-play} MAP estimate: with a Gaussian prior-proximal term of variance
$\delta^2$ around $\bmu_{t-1}$ and the Gaussian likelihood~\eqref{eq:model},
\begin{equation}
\x_{t-1} \;=\; \argmin_{\x}\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\delta^2}\,\|\x-\bmu_{t-1}\|^2}_{\text{prior proximal}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\sigma_y^2}\,\|\A\x-\K_{t-1}y\|^2}_{\text{data consistency}} .
\label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Substituting a \emph{classical} denoiser for that prox---e.g.\ a total-variation (Chambolle) step---recovers standard
plug-and-play HQS; we report this as the \textbf{TV} baseline in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Scale-matched measurement.}
Rather than driving every level with the raw $\y$, we \emph{scale-match} the measurement
to the current blur level. Because $\A$ and $\K_t$ commute~\eqref{eq:commute}, in the
noiseless case
\begin{equation}
\A\,\x_{t-1}\;=\;\A\,\K_{t-1}\,\x_0\;=\;\K_{t-1}\,\A\,\x_0\;=\;\K_{t-1}\,\y ,
\end{equation}
so the exact ``companion'' operator is $\mathbf{L}_{t-1}=\K_{t-1}$ and we set
\begin{equation}
\y_{t-1}\;=\;\mathbf{L}_{t-1}\,\y\;=\;\K_{t-1}\,\y .
\label{eq:scalematch}
\end{equation}
Early (large $t$) the target is heavily blurred, so data consistency is loose; as
$t\!\to\!0$, $\K_0=\I$ and $\y_0=\y$, enforcing the full measurement. This anneals data
consistency along the trajectory.

\paragraph{Closed form.}
Since the objective~\eqref{eq:map} is quadratic and \emph{both} terms are diagonal in
$\Phimat$,
the minimizer is available per frequency $j$ with no iteration:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\hat{\x}_{t-1}[j]
=\frac{w_p\,\hat{\bmu}_{t-1}[j]\;+\;w_y(t)\,\overline{\hat{a}[j]}\;\widehat{(\K_{t-1}\y)}[j]}
       {w_p\;+\;w_y(t)\,\lvert\hat{a}[j]\rvert^{2}} ,
\qquad \x_{t-1}=\Phimat^{-1}\hat{\x}_{t-1},
\;}
\label{eq:wiener}
\end{equation}
with weights
\begin{equation}
w_p=\frac{\alpha_p}{\delta^{2}},\qquad
w_y(t)=\frac{\alpha_y}{\sigma_y^{2}}\Bigl(1-\tfrac{t-1}{N}\Bigr),
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{(\cdot)}$ is complex conjugation. The scalars $\alpha_p,\alpha_y>0$ are
dimensionless weights on the prior and data terms; only their ratio $\alpha_p/\alpha_y$ shapes the filter,
$\delta$ is the prior-proximal standard deviation, and $\sigma_y$ the noise level.
Equation~\eqref{eq:wiener} is a per-frequency Wiener filter:
at frequencies with $|\hat a[j]|\!\approx\!0$
the estimate falls back to the prior $\hat{\bmu}[j]$, while at well-conditioned
frequencies it follows the data. The linear ramp in~\eqref{eq:weights} realizes the
``late'' schedule. No gradient is taken through
$R_\theta$; the network is used only as the prior-proximal map producing $\bmu_{t-1}$.

\paragraph{Frequency-aware regularization (speckle control).}
The Wiener filter~\eqref{eq:wiener} amplifies measurement noise most where the operator is
weakest. Its per-frequency noise gain is
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{n}}[j]=\frac{w_y\,\lvert\hat a[j]\rvert\,\sigma_y}{w_p+w_y\,\lvert\hat a[j]\rvert^{2}},
\label{eq:noisegain}
\end{equation}
which peaks at $\lvert\hat a[j]\rvert=\sqrt{w_p/w_y}$ with height $\tfrac12\sigma_y\sqrt{w_y/w_p}$.
Because we up-weight data as $w_y\!\propto\!1/\sigma_y^{2}$, this peak is essentially
\emph{independent of $\sigma_y$}: even at small noise it injects a fixed-amplitude, structured
high-frequency ripple---visible as \emph{speckle}, and most severe when $\A$ has a genuine null
space (e.g.\ the anti-aliased downsampling of super-resolution, whose transfer vanishes above the
low-resolution Nyquist).

We suppress it by making the prior precision frequency-dependent: add a DCT-diagonal operator
$\mathbf R=\Phimat^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(r[j]\bigr)\Phimat$ to the prior proximal,
\begin{equation}
\x_{t-1}=\argmin_{\x}\;\tfrac12\bigl(\x-\bmu_{t-1}\bigr)^{\!\top}\!\bigl(w_p\I+\mathbf R\bigr)\bigl(\x-\bmu_{t-1}\bigr)
\;+\;\frac{1}{2\sigma_y^2}\bigl\|\A\x-\K_{t-1}\y\bigr\|^{2},
\quad
r[j]=\gamma\,w_p\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\lvert\hat a[j]\rvert}{\lvert\hat a[0]\rvert}\Bigr)_{\!+},
\label{eq:reg}
\end{equation}
with $(\cdot)_+=\max(\cdot,0)$. The weight $r[j]$ is zero in the pass band
($\lvert\hat a[j]\rvert\!\approx\!\lvert\hat a[0]\rvert$) and rises to $\gamma w_p$ where the operator
is blind ($\hat a[j]\!\to\!0$): it trusts the prior exactly where the measurement carries no
information---the range/null-space split of DDRM. The minimizer keeps the closed
form~\eqref{eq:wiener} with $w_p\!\mapsto\!w_p+r[j]$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\hat{\x}_{t-1}[j]
=\frac{\bigl(w_p+r[j]\bigr)\,\hat{\bmu}_{t-1}[j]\;+\;w_y(t)\,\overline{\hat a[j]}\;\widehat{(\K_{t-1}\y)}[j]}
       {w_p+r[j]\;+\;w_y(t)\,\lvert\hat a[j]\rvert^{2}} .
\;}
\label{eq:wienerreg}
\end{equation}
Adding $r[j]$ to the denominator flattens the noise-gain peak~\eqref{eq:noisegain} in the weak band
while leaving the well-measured low frequencies ($r\!\approx\!0$) untouched. For non-diagonal $\A$
the same term enters the spatial CG system as
$\bigl(w_p\I+\mathbf R+w_y\mathbf H^{\!\top}\mathbf H\bigr)\x=\bigl(w_p\I+\mathbf R\bigr)\bmu_{t-1}+w_y\mathbf H^{\!\top}\y_{t-1}$,
with $\lvert\hat a[j]\rvert^{2}$ taken from a Hutchinson estimate of
$\operatorname{diag}(\A^{\!\top}\A)$ in the DCT basis. Setting $\gamma\!=\!0$
recovers~\eqref{eq:wiener}; we use $\gamma\!=\!0.5$ by default for deblur (a small, near
distortion-free speckle clean). The reg's value is regime-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:regablation}).
It pays off when the data term dominates and transition-band \emph{speckle}---not measurement
noise---is the limiting error: motion and low-noise super-resolution ($\gamma\!\approx\!0.5$). As the
noise grows the optimal $\gamma$ shrinks to $0$, since the error is then genuine noise the reg cannot
remove.

\paragraph{Basis selection and the boundary question.}
For Gaussian/heat $\A$, $\Phimat=$ DCT and $\hat a$ is real, so $\A$ shares the prior's
Neumann eigenbasis and the closed form~\eqref{eq:wiener} is exact. For motion (and other
kernels not diagonalized by the DCT) one \emph{could} move the whole pipeline to the DFT basis
with the periodic heat symbol
$\hat k_t[j]=\exp(-\tfrac12\sigma_t^2\lvert\omega_j\rvert^2)$, $\omega_j=2\pi\,\mathrm{fftfreq}$,
which formally restores $\A\K_t=\K_t\A$ and the diagonal closed form~\eqref{eq:wiener}. In
practice this is the wrong trade: it imposes \emph{periodic} boundaries on non-periodic images,
so the inverse-DFT data step rings at the border (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). We therefore do
\emph{not} train a separate DFT-basis prior. Instead we keep the single DCT/Neumann prior and,
whenever $\A$ is not DCT-diagonal, solve the data step in the \emph{spatial} domain by conjugate
gradient with the true reflect-boundary operator $\mathbf H$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:cg}). CG uses only
$\mathbf H\!\cdot$ and $\mathbf H^{\!\top}\!\cdot$ and never diagonalizes $\A$, so it needs
\emph{no} shared eigenbasis between $\A$ and the prior: basis-matching is unnecessary. The
scale-match~\eqref{eq:scalematch} then only requires $\A\K_t\approx\K_t\A$: exact for Gaussian in
the DCT basis, and accurate to $\sim\!10^{-3}$ for reflect-boundary motion. The reflect/Neumann
convention is also the physically correct one for natural-image boundaries.

Table~\ref{tab:params} is the full parameter sheet. Only the ratio $\alpha_p/\alpha_y$

\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algo}

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{HQS restoration with a non-hot diffusion prior}
\label{alg:hqs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require measurement $\y$; operator $\A$ (transfer $\hat a$ in basis $\Phimat$);
prior $R_\theta$ and heat symbols $\hat k_t$; weights $w_p, w_y, \sigma_y$; levels $N$
\State $\x \gets \Phimat^{-1}\!\big(\hat k_N \odot \Phimat\y\big)$
\Comment{initialize: blur $\y$ to the top level}
\State $\hat{\y}\gets \Phimat\y$
\For{$t = N, N\!-\!1, \dots, 1$}
  \State $\bmu \gets R_\theta(\x,\,t)$
  \Comment{learned reverse step (image domain) $\to$ prior mean $\bmu_{t-1}$}
  \State $\widehat{\y}_{t-1} \gets \hat k_{t-1}\odot\hat{\y}$
  \Comment{scale-matched measurement $\K_{t-1}\y$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:scalematch}}
  \State $w_y(t) \gets \dfrac{\alpha_y}{\sigma_y^2}\Big(1-\dfrac{t-1}{N}\Big)$
  \State $\displaystyle \hat{\x}\gets
     \frac{w_p\,(\Phimat\bmu)\;+\;w_y(t)\,\overline{\hat a}\odot\widehat{\y}_{t-1}}
          {w_p\;+\;w_y(t)\,|\hat a|^2}$
  \Comment{closed-form MAP, Eq.~\eqref{eq:wiener}}
  \State $\x \gets \mathrm{clip}\big(\Phimat^{-1}\hat{\x},\,-1,\,1\big)$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\x$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Each iteration is one network evaluation plus two transforms; the data step is
$\mathcal{O}(d\log d)$ and exact. The method never differentiates the prior.

\section{Numerical results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Held-out CelebA-HQ-256, $n=200$ images (the first 200 of the standard validation split); every method
reconstructs the \emph{identical} set from the same observations, with noise $\sigma_y\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20\}$
(SR: $\{0.01,0.05\}$). Because DPS is a $\sim$1000-NFE sampler, it is evaluated on the first 100 of these
200 (a strict subset). Priors: \emph{COLD-DIFF} and \emph{IHDM-211M}. Baselines:
Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS), DDRM (separable operators only), DiffPIR (NFE${=}100$,
FFT data consistency), and DDNM (super-res). Metrics:
PSNR$\uparrow$ (reported as mean$\pm$std), SSIM$\uparrow$, LPIPS$\downarrow$, measurement consistency
$\mathrm{MC}=\|\y-\A\hat\x\|/\|\y\|\;\downarrow$.

\paragraph{Gaussian / heat deblur.}
With $\A$ a DCT-diagonal Gaussian blur, $\A$ and the heat prior
share the DCT basis, so~\eqref{eq:wiener} is exact. The non-hot HQS prior leads distortion at
low-to-moderate noise (Table~\ref{tab:gauss}); the operator-matched DDRM is competitive and edges
ahead at the highest noise ($\sigma_y{=}0.20$), while the diffusion baselines win perceptual LPIPS
(DiffPIR best). On the non-separable motion/defocus operators, where DDRM does not apply, IHDM leads
distortion across all noise levels.

\paragraph{Motion deblur.}
A Levin09 kernel~\#1 is \emph{asymmetric}, so it is not diagonalized by the DCT; and on a finite
image with a realistic \textbf{reflect boundary}---the standard blur-synthesis
convention---the blur operator is block
Toeplitz-plus-Hankel, \emph{not} circulant, so no single transform diagonalizes it. A closed-form
\emph{frequency-domain} data step (the Wiener update~\eqref{eq:wiener}) would apply an inverse DFT
over the whole image and thereby re-impose periodic boundaries, producing border ringing on
realistic data. We therefore keep the same per-step MAP objective~\eqref{eq:map} but solve its data
step in the \emph{spatial} domain by conjugate gradient. Writing $\y=\mathbf H\x_0+\bm n$ with
$\mathbf H$ the reflect-boundary convolution matrix, each step solves the normal equations
\begin{equation}
\big(w_p\I + w_y\,\mathbf H^{\!\top}\mathbf H\big)\,\x \;=\; w_p\bmu + w_y\,\mathbf H^{\!\top}(\K_{t-1}\y)
\label{eq:cg}
\end{equation}
by CG. No iFFT over the image is formed, so there is no periodic wrap and no ringing, and
the cost is negligible next to the network evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:motion} reports every method
on identical reflect-boundary observations. To isolate the prior, the TV baseline uses the
\emph{same} spatial-CG data step as the learned priors so IHDM$+$CG, cold$+$CG and TV all recover
the full frame without ringing; only DPS, whose guidance uses a circular forward, inherits the boundary ring.

\paragraph{Defocus deblur.}
Out-of-focus blur is convolution with a uniform \textbf{disk} (pillbox) point-spread function; we
use radius $7$ ($15\times15$) and the same realistic reflect-boundary synthesis as motion. Unlike
the motion kernel, a disk is \emph{symmetric} in both axes, and a both-axes-symmetric kernel under
reflect boundaries is exactly diagonalized by the DCT-II---the very basis the Neumann heat prior
uses. We confirm this by extracting the disk's DCT transfer $\hat a$ with a single probe
($\hat a=\mathrm{DCT}(\A\,\mathrm{IDCT}(\mathbf 1))$) and checking
$\lVert\A\x-\mathrm{IDCT}(\hat a\odot\mathrm{DCT}\,\x)\rVert/\lVert\A\x\rVert\approx9\times10^{-6}$;
correspondingly the intertwining $\A\K_t=\K_t\A$ holds to $\sim\!10^{-15}$ (vs $\sim\!10^{-3}$ for
the asymmetric motion kernel). Defocus therefore needs \emph{no} spatial CG: it uses the same
closed-form per-frequency DCT-Wiener data step~\eqref{eq:wiener} as Gaussian---exact and
iteration-free. This is the general rule: \textbf{symmetric operators} (Gaussian, defocus; also
symmetric anti-alias filters) admit the closed-form DCT-HQS step, whereas \textbf{asymmetric
operators} (motion) require spatial CG. Table~\ref{tab:defocus} reports the full comparison across
noise. IHDM$+$HQS is the best full-frame method at every level ($23.04\!\to\!27.97$\,dB at
$\sigma_y=0.05$, $+4.9$\,dB), beating the TV prior by $1.7$\,dB and DPS by $4.4$\,dB on distortion;
the diffusion baselines again win perceptual LPIPS (DiffPIR best). The closed-form solve matches the spatial-CG result to
$0.01$\,dB, as it must.

\paragraph{Super-resolution and a DDRM baseline.}
Super-resolution ($\times4$) uses the operator $\A=\text{avg-pool}_4\!\circ\!B_{\text{aa}}$: an
anti-alias Gaussian blur followed by area decimation. Unlike the deblur operators, $\A$ has a
genuine \emph{null space} (its transfer vanishes above the LR Nyquist), so the plain data step
amplifies noise into transition-band \emph{speckle}, and the frequency-aware
reg~\eqref{eq:wienerreg} is doing real work. We compare against \textbf{DDRM} (pre-trained FFHQ
diffusion, \texttt{ffhq\_10m}) on the \emph{same} operator. Because $\A=H_{1}\!\otimes\!H_{1}$ is
separable, its SVD factors as a Kronecker product of the $1$-D SVD---exactly the structure DDRM's SVD
replacement needs---so DDRM inverts our operator to machine precision (the exact $H_1$ matches $\A$
to $4\times10^{-15}$). Table~\ref{tab:sr} reports both at matched absolute noise
($\sigma_0=\sigma_y/2$). SMDC$+$IHDM leads PSNR/SSIM at low noise ($\sigma_y{=}0.01$) while DDRM,
with its stochastic hot-diffusion prior, edges ahead on PSNR/SSIM at $\sigma_y{=}0.05$; the perceptual
LPIPS goes to the guidance baselines (DiffPIR at $\sigma_y{=}0.01$, PiGDM at $\sigma_y{=}0.05$)---the
same distortion/perception split we see against DPS.
Crucially this comparison is \emph{only} possible for separable operators: the Levin motion kernel
and the disk defocus PSF are non-separable, so their full SVD is intractable and DDRM does not apply
(the ``--'' rows in Tables~\ref{tab:motion},~\ref{tab:defocus}).

To meet DDRM on \emph{its} home ground we also add the \emph{pure} box downsample (avg-pool, no
anti-alias), i.e.\ DDRM's native \texttt{sr4} degradation (Table~\ref{tab:sr}, lower block). Dropping
the anti-alias pre-blur destroys less high-frequency content, so every method starts from a sharper
bicubic anchor ($27.34$ vs $23.58$\,dB at $\sigma_y{=}0.01$) and the null space is milder. The
distortion/perception split still persists: SMDC$+$IHDM leads PSNR/SSIM at low noise ($+0.80$\,dB
over DDRM at $\sigma_y{=}0.01$), the operator-matched DDNM edges it out on distortion at
$\sigma_y{=}0.05$, and the guidance baselines win LPIPS (DiffPIR at $\sigma_y{=}0.01$, PiGDM at
$\sigma_y{=}0.05$). The reg's role also flips with the operator:
on the milder box null space the low-noise cell prefers $\gamma{=}0$ (flat, noise-free), whereas at
$\sigma_y{=}0.05$ the box's poorer transition band makes speckle control pay off strongly---the sweep
is unimodal with a broad peak at $\gamma{=}4$ ($27.35\!\to\!\mathbf{27.70}$\,dB, LPIPS
$0.353\!\to\!0.320$, SSIM $0.772\!\to\!0.795$; turning over only past $\gamma{=}8$)---a far larger
payoff than the anti-alias operator's flat $\gamma$-plateau, exactly because the box is a poorer
low-pass with more transition-band noise to suppress.

\paragraph{Scale-matched vs.\ naive data consistency (the title ablation).}
Every result above uses the \emph{scale-matched} data step: at reverse level $t$ the measurement is
carried to the current blur level by the companion $\mathbf{L}_{t-1}=\K_{t-1}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:scalematch}), so the per-step residual $\A\x_{t-1}-\K_{t-1}\y$ compares two quantities
that live at the \emph{same} scale $\sigma_{t-1}$. The naming contribution of this paper is exactly
this companion. To isolate it we run the identical solver, prior, schedule, and reg, changing a
\emph{single line}: the data step now enforces consistency against the raw, full-resolution
measurement $\y$ at every step ($\A\x_{t-1}-\y$)---the ``obvious'' choice, and what plug-and-play
methods do when they inject $\y$ into a per-step gradient without accounting for the coarseness of the
intermediate state. Table~\ref{tab:dcablation} reports both on the Gaussian-deblur cells at $n{=}200$.

The effect is not a tuning detail but a \textbf{structural collapse}: naive DC falls to
$\approx\!12$\,dB across all noise levels---a $13$--$15$\,dB drop from scale-matched, and $6$--$11$\,dB
\emph{below the blurry input itself}. The reason is exact: the reverse state $\x_{t-1}$ is blurred to
scale $\sigma_{t-1}$, so $\A\x_{t-1}$ is \emph{even blurrier} than the observation and cannot equal
the sharp $\y$. The per-step MAP then chases an infeasible target and diverges, injecting the
high-frequency content of a naive deconvolution at every one of the $\sim\!200$ steps. Scale-matching is what makes the per-step likelihood
\emph{consistent}---$\A\x_{t-1}=\K_{t-1}\y$ is exactly solvable precisely because $\A$ and $\K_{t-1}$
commute (Eq.~\ref{eq:scalematch})---and it is load-bearing rather than incremental. Tellingly, naive
DC is no better on the very quantity it optimizes: its measurement consistency MC does not improve
(and worsens once the step size is opened up, below), because the divergence spoils the fit it was
trying to enforce.

This is \emph{not} an artifact of a bad step size. Sweeping the naive data step's weight
$w_{\text{data}}\!\in\![0.25,64]$ and reg $\gamma\!\in\![0,2]$ on the tuning split, its best case is
only $13.01$\,dB (at $w_{\text{data}}{=}0.25,\gamma{=}0$)---gentler consistency helps by under a dB
and never approaches feasibility. Run at $n{=}200$ this best-tuned naive DC reaches
$13.26\pmstd{0.82}$\,dB, still $\approx\!14$\,dB below scale-matched (and with a \emph{worse}
measurement consistency, $\text{MC}{=}0.45$ vs.\ $0.09$). We therefore report the naive arm with
the \emph{matched} hyper-parameters (a true one-variable ablation) in Table~\ref{tab:dcablation}; the
re-tuned figure only confirms the gap survives even when the baseline is tuned on the eval set. The
complementary alternative---applying data consistency to a clean-image estimate $\hat\x_0$ rather
than to $\x_{t-1}$, as DPS and DiffPIR do---is a \emph{different} method, already tabulated above: it
avoids the divergence but forgoes the exact per-step likelihood and closed-form data step that
scale-matching buys.

\paragraph{On the prior: why non-hot.}
The ablation above isolates the \emph{solver}; the \emph{prior} axis is settled by the main tables
together with a structural fact. Scale-matched data consistency is not merely well-suited to a
non-hot prior---it is \emph{only defined} for one. The exact per-step target
$\A\x_{t-1}=\K_{t-1}\y$ exists because the prior's forward blur $\K_t$ commutes with the measurement
$\A$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:commute}); a hot variance-preserving (VP) prior injects white noise rather than
blurring, so no companion $\mathbf{L}_t$ exists and the method necessarily reduces to
$\hat\x_0$-guidance. Every \emph{learned} baseline in Tables~\ref{tab:gauss}--\ref{tab:sr}---DPS,
DDRM, DiffPIR, DDNM---is exactly such a hot VP prior (the shared FFHQ \texttt{ffhq\_10m} model)
driven by the strongest guidance available for that prior class, and our non-hot IHDM$+$SMDC leads
them on distortion throughout. We therefore do not run a separate hot-prior arm of \emph{our} solver:
there is none to run---a hot prior forgoes the exact per-step likelihood by construction, and
Table~\ref{tab:dcablation} shows precisely what happens to a per-step data step once the scale match
it depends on is removed. A fully controlled swap---the same blurring forward made \emph{hot} by added
noise (blurring diffusion)---requires retraining the prior and is left to future work. The present
evidence already isolates the prior claim: the structural non-existence of $\mathbf{L}_t$ under VP,
plus the measured gap to the hot-prior baselines on identical data and operators.

\paragraph{Computational cost.}
Table~\ref{tab:compute} reports the compute of every method under two lenses: \emph{NFE} (neural
function evaluations---the hardware-independent count of prior-network passes) and wall-clock
seconds per image on a single TITAN RTX (restoration only, batch~1). The point is not that our method
is the fastest in raw seconds---it is not: DDRM, DDNM and DiffPIR run a \emph{smaller} $94$M VP prior
at $20$--$100$ steps and finish in $1$--$7$\,s. The point is where the cost \emph{goes}. Our
scale-matched data step is closed-form (a per-frequency division, Eq.~\ref{eq:wiener}) or a $12$-iter
CG with no network call, so it adds \textbf{zero NFE}: our runtime is exactly that of an
unconditional $N{=}200$-step IHDM sampling pass, and the data consistency is free. This is the
opposite of guidance-based DPS, which spends $1000$ steps \emph{and} backpropagates through the prior
at every step (two network passes per step). On the identical GPU it costs $95.5$\,s/img---$\sim\!3\times$
our $25$--$36$\,s and $5\times$ the NFE---for uniformly lower distortion
(Tables~\ref{tab:gauss}--\ref{tab:defocus}). Against the fast SVD methods the trade is qualitative,
not temporal: DDRM and DDNM diagonalize $\A$ by an SVD that exists \emph{only} for separable
operators, so they are simply inapplicable to motion and defocus (blank rows above), whereas our
closed-form step follows from the prior's own heat basis and applies to every operator we test. Our
two remaining cost levers are orthogonal to the contribution and quantified elsewhere: the $211$M
prior (vs.\ $94$M) is a backbone choice, and the $200$-step budget is the IHDM default that can be
reduced without retraining (a step-count/quality sweep is left to future work).

\paragraph{Steps vs.\ quality (NFE sweep).}
The $N{=}200$ operating point used above is the IHDM default, not a tuned choice. Because our data
step is closed-form at every level, the blur-level grid can be subsampled to any $M<N$ steps with no
other change to the method---a respaced, strictly-decreasing schedule with $\text{NFE}{=}M$, the
closed-form MAP still applied at each retained level. Table~\ref{tab:nfe} sweeps $M$ on Gaussian
deblur at $n{=}200$. Degradation is smooth and monotone: halving to $M{=}100$ costs only $1.1$\,dB,
and $M{=}25$---an $8\times$ NFE and $\sim\!6\times$ wall-clock reduction---still retains $25.24$\,dB.
Two features stand out. First, the loss is almost entirely \emph{perceptual, not data-fidelity}:
measurement consistency MC is essentially flat across the sweep ($0.091\!\rightarrow\!0.102$) while
LPIPS rises, exactly as expected when the closed-form data step (applied at every retained level,
independent of $M$) carries consistency and the prior supplies high-frequency detail. Second, the
low-NFE regime is strikingly cheap: at $M{=}12$ ($\sim\!2$\,s/img) IHDM already scores $24.73$\,dB,
\emph{above} DPS's $24.12$\,dB---which spends $1000$ NFE at $95.5$\,s/img (Table~\ref{tab:compute})---so
our method matches or beats the guidance baseline at a few percent of its compute. The same monotone
curve holds for the other operators and \emph{both} solver types (first-$16$: motion, the spatial-CG
path, $30.43\!\rightarrow\!27.12$\,dB and defocus $27.82\!\rightarrow\!25.21$\,dB from $M{=}200$ to
$M{=}25$), so the trend is not Gaussian-specific. The $200$-step default is therefore a quality
ceiling, not a requirement; a practitioner can move down the curve smoothly to trade fidelity for speed.

\paragraph{Robustness to noise-level misspecification.}
A practical solver rarely knows the true measurement noise $\sigma_y$. Where it enters is
structurally different across methods, and that difference governs how they fail. In our method
$\sigma_y$ appears \emph{only} as the soft data weight $w_y=\alpha_y/\sigma_y^2$ in the per-step
MAP~\eqref{eq:map}; the prior itself is \emph{noise-unconditioned} (it is a heat-dissipation model
conditioned on blur level, not on noise). In DDRM the same quantity sets the SVD spectral
shrinkage---each mode is divided by a $\sigma_0$-dependent factor---so it is baked into the estimator,
not a soft weight. Table~\ref{tab:robust} feeds both methods the \emph{true} noise in the data but a
\emph{wrong} assumed $\sigma_y$ (a misspecification factor $\times0.5$ to $\times4$), holding
everything else fixed.

Three things stand out. \textbf{(i)} The correct $\sigma_y$ is optimal for both---robustness is not
independence---but the penalty for a moderate error is small for ours: within $\times2$ either way IHDM
stays within $\sim\!1.5$\,dB at low/moderate noise. \textbf{(ii)} The fragile directions are
\emph{opposite}. DDRM tolerates over-estimation but \textbf{collapses catastrophically under
\emph{under}-estimation}: a mere $\times0.5$ error drops it to $4.98$\,dB \emph{at every noise level}
(the reconstruction is $94\%$ saturated noise---dividing the SVD by a too-small $\sigma_0$ amplifies
the measurement without bound). IHDM tolerates under-estimation ($\le\!1.5$\,dB) and is instead
softest under gross \emph{over}-estimation at high noise. \textbf{(iii)} Crucially, the two failure
\emph{modes} differ in kind: IHDM \textbf{never diverges}---its worst cell ($\times4$ over at
$\sigma_y{=}0.20$) degrades \emph{gracefully} to $18.3$\,dB, i.e.\ back to the blurry-input level as
the data term is starved and the prior takes over, whereas DDRM's worst case is unbounded noise
amplification. Because a noise-unconditioned prior turns $\sigma_y$ into a bounded interpolation
knob rather than a divisor, IHDM has \emph{no catastrophic regime}: under any noise
\emph{under}-estimate it stays usable while DDRM does not, making it the safer choice when $\sigma_y$
is uncertain.

\paragraph{Frequency-aware reg ablation.}
Table~\ref{tab:regablation} sweeps the reg $\gamma$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:wienerreg}) per degradation. The
only \emph{large distortion} gain is \textbf{motion} ($+0.66$\,dB PSNR, $+0.036$ SSIM at
$\sigma_y{=}0.05$), whose asymmetric reflect operator leaves a genuine weak band the prior fills.
Elsewhere the reg is at best a near distortion-free \emph{perceptual} clean-up on a flat PSNR
plateau. \textbf{Gaussian} is flat to $\pm0.01$\,dB across $\gamma\in[0.1,0.5]$ while LPIPS falls
monotonically ($0.362\!\to\!0.356$, optimum $\gamma\!\approx\!0.4$); \textbf{low-noise SR} likewise
holds a flat plateau ($27.11$\,dB across $\gamma\in[0,0.4]$) with LPIPS improving ($0.359\!\to\!0.353$)
for a $0.00$\,dB cost. The reg is monotonically \emph{harmful} where the operator is (nearly)
full-rank or measurement noise---not speckle---dominates: \textbf{defocus} (every $\gamma>0$ hurts,
$-0.01$ to $-0.13$\,dB) and \textbf{higher-noise SR} ($-0.18$\,dB at $\sigma_y{=}0.05$).

The lone \emph{distortion} exception is the \textbf{pure box downsample}. Stripping the anti-alias
pre-blur leaves a poorer low-pass with a real transition band, so at $\sigma_y{=}0.05$ the reg buys a
genuine, unimodal $+0.35$\,dB (peak $\gamma{=}4$: $27.35\!\to\!27.70$\,dB, LPIPS $0.353\!\to\!0.320$,
SSIM $0.772\!\to\!0.795$; it turns over past $\gamma{=}8$). The reg's payoff thus tracks how much
transition-band noise the operator admits. Hence the per-noise operating points
(Table~\ref{tab:regablation2}): $\gamma$ \emph{shrinks} with noise for Gaussian ($0.25\!\to\!0$) and
motion ($0.5\!\to\!0$), stays $0$ for defocus, and \emph{rises} ($0\!\to\!4$) for box-SR alone---whose
weak band is a contiguous prior-filled cutoff rather than the active notches of motion.

\paragraph{Beyond ill-conditioned operators: inpainting.}
Every operator above is full-support and \emph{ill-conditioned} (blur, downsample), the regime where
scale-matching is essential. Inpainting---a $0/1$ pixel mask $\mathbf M$---is the opposite: an
orthogonal projection (singular values exactly $0/1$), well-conditioned but rank-deficient, and it
lies formally \emph{outside} our intertwining. A mask does not commute with the heat blur
($\mathbf M\K_t\neq\K_t\mathbf M$), so no exact companion $\mathbf L_t$ exists---the blurred-then-masked
measurement depends on the unobserved pixels. We approximate the missing companion by a \emph{re-blur}
step: at level $t$ we composite the observed data with the current hole estimate, blur the composite to
level $t$, and pin the observed region to it, so the injected data lives at the state's own scale
rather than as sharp values in a blurry field. A few RePaint-style resampling passes per level
($U{=}5$) then let the observed data propagate into the holes. Algorithm~\ref{alg:inpaint} gives the
full loop; note it differs structurally from Algorithm~\ref{alg:hqs}---there is no closed-form MAP
data step (a mask admits no companion $\mathbf L_t$), only the re-blur pin plus the inner resampling
loop that Algorithm~\ref{alg:hqs} does not have.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Inpainting via the re-blur companion with RePaint resampling}
\label{alg:inpaint}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require masked measurement $\y$ (holes zeroed); mask $\mathbf M$ ($1$=observed, $0$=hole); prior
reverse step $R_\theta$; heat blur $\K_t$ and symbols $\hat k_t$ in basis $\Phimat$; levels $N$;
resampling passes $U$
\State $\bar y \gets (\mathbf 1^{\!\top}\y)\,/\,(\mathbf 1^{\!\top}\mathbf M)$
\Comment{mean of the observed pixels}
\State $\x \gets \mathrm{clip}\big(\K_N\!\big(\y + (\mathbf 1-\mathbf M)\,\bar y\big)\big)$
\Comment{fill holes, blur to the top level}
\For{$t = N, N\!-\!1, \dots, 1$}
  \For{$u = 1, \dots, U$}
    \State $\bmu \gets R_\theta(\x,\,t)$
    \Comment{prior deblurs one level, $t\!\to\!t\!-\!1$}
    \State $\mathbf c \gets \y + (\mathbf 1-\mathbf M)\odot\bmu$
    \Comment{composite: observed data $+$ current hole estimate}
    \State $\x \gets \mathrm{clip}\big(\mathbf M\odot\K_{t-1}\mathbf c \;+\; (\mathbf 1-\mathbf M)\odot\bmu\big)$
    \Comment{re-blur companion: pin the observed region at level $t\!-\!1$}
    \If{$u < U$}
      \State $\x \gets \mathrm{clip}\big(\Phimat^{-1}\big[(\hat k_t/\hat k_{t-1})\odot\Phimat\x\big]\big)$
      \Comment{re-blur up to level $t$ and resample}
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State \Return $\x$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The outcome (Table~\ref{tab:inpaint}) is sharply regime-dependent. On \emph{random} masks the method is
strong and robust across densities: at $50\%$ missing it reaches $29.9$\,dB hole-PSNR / LPIPS $0.030$
with \emph{zero} failures over $50$ images, and still recovers $28.7$\,dB at $70\%$ missing. On an
identical fixed $50\%$ mask it \emph{beats} DDRM on every metric (hole-PSNR $29.97$ vs.\ $27.70$, SSIM
$0.941$ vs.\ $0.871$, LPIPS $0.029$ vs.\ $0.084$). The limitation is equally clear and instructive: a
large \emph{contiguous} hole (a centered $37.5\%$ box) fails ($10.7$\,dB), because no observed pixel
lies near the hole interior for the re-blur to reach, and a heat-dissipation prior \emph{deblurs}
rather than \emph{synthesizes}. Inpainting thus succeeds precisely where the mask is scattered enough
for diffusion to bridge the gaps; wide-baseline hole-filling needs a generative (VP/blurring-diffusion)
prior and is left to future work. The price is the resampling: $U{=}5\times$ the NFE ($\sim\!1000$).

\paragraph{Summary.}
For \textbf{Gaussian} blur, the measurement operator and the heat prior share the DCT
eigenbasis, so the non-hot HQS prior gives an exact, backprop-free, closed-form data step and is
the best distortion method---beating both a well-tuned classical baseline (TV) and DPS
(Table~\ref{tab:gauss}). For \textbf{motion} blur under realistic reflect boundaries the operator
is not diagonalized by any fast transform, so a closed-form frequency-domain (iFFT) data step
re-imposes periodic boundaries and rings; we instead solve the same per-step MAP objective with a
spatial \textbf{conjugate-gradient} data step (the true reflect operator $\mathbf H$,
Eq.~\ref{eq:cg}), which eliminates the border ring and makes IHDM$+$CG the best full-frame method
at every noise level (Table~\ref{tab:motion}).

Two caveats keep the picture honest. (i) \textbf{TV is a strong classical prior}: given the
\emph{same} data step as the learned prior (the closed-form DCT solve on Gaussian, the spatial-CG
solve on motion), a TV regularizer comes within $\sim\!1$--$1.8$\,dB on both full frames, so the gain
over a well-tuned classical prox is real but modest. (ii) \textbf{DPS} is the most robust choice when
the operator structure is unknown or perceptual LPIPS is the target, since it never diagonalizes
$\A$ and needs only $\A,\A^\top$. The learned diffusion prior wins where its structural assumptions
hold; TV and DPS are the baselines to beat elsewhere.
